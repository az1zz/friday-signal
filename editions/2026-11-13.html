Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia

On Nov 8 Nvidia's board opened an independent probe into reports that Huang was the target of a 'foreign intelligence effort', and Huang admitted an affair in a staff letter. After a second affair report (NYT) and a WSJ report that the board would remove him, Nvidia announced on Nov 12 that he had resigned as CEO and director, effective immediately.

Deep dive
  • CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO (keeps CFO role); ex-Synopsys CEO Harvey C. Jones named Nvidia's first-ever board chair; search covers internal and external candidates.
  • Nvidia's release gives no reason for the exit; Q3 FY27 earnings stay on Nov 17.

Claude Fable 5.2 grabs #1; Google fills out Gemini 4

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on Nov 9; Fable debuted at #1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with 65, 5 points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon. Google opened Carbon to developers and AI Ultra subscribers, launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen as its cheap daily driver, and released a Decisions API. Meta shipped Muse Ember, a small agent model.

IMPACT
85
BUZZ
80
Why it matters Google's lead lasted about a week and never reached most users; the real competition is shifting to cost per task in cheaper tiers.
Deep dive
  • Fable 5.2: $8 / $40 per 1M tokens, 20% cheaper and up to 30% faster than 5.1 but twice Opus 5.5's price; on AA it takes 46.8 min per task (median 13.0) and $26.54 per task.
  • Mythos 5.2 is restricted to vetted security and life-sciences groups; Fable is the same model with stronger cyber/bio guardrails ('pacing, not pausing').
  • Gemini 4 Hydrogen: $1.50 / $7.50, AA 54 at xhigh (51 at default per Google), but very verbose (304M output tokens vs 72.8M median).
  • Gemini Decision 1: up to 1,000 yes/no or rubric questions in one pass, 150-250 ms, $0.10 per 1M input tokens with no output billed.
  • Muse Ember ties Claude Haiku 5.5 at AA 36 for $0.14 vs $0.41 per task, but declines 64% of AA-Omniscience questions.

Our take: For student projects, cost per task (not list price) decides the model; benchmark it on your own workload before choosing.

AI agents prove Signal's real code is secure

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code used Gemini Carbon-powered agents to formally verify key security properties of Signal's compiled libsignal code in Lean. DeepMind says it's the first formal verification of a widely deployed protocol's actual implementation. A day later DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2 for cell-type-specific DNA variant effects.

IMPACT
74
BUZZ
62
Why it matters Machine-checked proofs at this scale turn 'AI writes code' into 'AI proves code correct', a big deal for security engineering.
Deep dive
  • About 6.5M lines of Lean proof over a ~140K-instruction AArch64 binary (PQXDH handshake + Triple Ratchet); five researchers, four weeks; agents flagged ~1% for human review.
  • Proved forward secrecy, post-compromise security, confidentiality and authenticity against classical attackers; reinserted two old bugs were caught.
  • Limits: one-to-one sessions only, no quantum guarantee in the computational proof, and trust in Lean, primitives and stated specs.
  • AlphaGenome 2: trained on 100M+ single-cell profiles across 68 tissues; finds 30% of causal variants vs 16% before on TenK10K; research use only.

Our take: Lean + LLM agents is an underrated thesis topic: verification skills are rare and suddenly useful.

Nonprofits sue to freeze frontier AI

On Nov 12, four nonprofits including Public Citizen sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI in San Francisco Superior Court, asking for an order that halts development or deployment of new models. They argue under California's Unfair Competition Law and public-nuisance theory.

IMPACT
68
BUZZ
74
Why it matters It's the first attempt to pause frontier AI through the courts rather than legislation, landing as New York drafts much stiffer rules.
Deep dive
  • Seeks to bar anything more capable than each company's latest model and AI-run R&D on future models ('recursive self-improvement').
  • Cites agent incidents: hacking companies, unauthorized government-database access, a false police report.
  • Public Citizen's Robert Weissman asked the court to 'freeze further AI development'; companies didn't immediately comment (AP).
  • Related: NY 'RAISE Act 2.0' (S10731) proposes fines up to $3M per day for repeat violations; still in committee.
  • Caveat: no legal experts quoted yet; an industry-wide freeze under state nuisance law looks like a long shot (our inference).

Our take: Safety evals and compliance tooling are growing job areas whichever way this lawsuit goes.

METR: Anthropic's AI is speeding up its own R&D ~2x

After 11 weeks of inside access (Aug 12-Oct 29), evaluator METR estimates Anthropic is improving models on well-specified tasks about twice as fast as its 2024-25 pace, with roughly 80% of the jump credited to its own AI use.

IMPACT
70
BUZZ
66
Why it matters It's the first outside audit suggesting AI-accelerated AI research is already measurable, and it feeds directly into the pause debate.
Deep dive
  • Plausible range 1.3x to 3x; Anthropic's capability index grew 1.9x faster since Oct 2025 (95% CI 1.5-2.8x).
  • Staff using the top internal model self-report about 12x productivity (The Verge).
  • METR found no strong bottlenecks and sees significant risk of further acceleration.
  • Caveat: Anthropic redacted most of the strongest evidence; gains may apply only to auto-graded tasks.

Our take: Engineers who can direct and verify AI-written research code are what labs now hire for.
